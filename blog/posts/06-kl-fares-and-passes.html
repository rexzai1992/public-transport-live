Fares in the Klang Valley are cheap and distance-based, but the passes are where people overpay or underpay. Here's the plain version. Exact sen-by-sen prices change, so treat the figures below as the shape of it and confirm the latest on the operators' own sites.

{{photo:photo-kl-faregates|Fare gates at Pasar Seni station, Kuala Lumpur}}

The one rule that saves you money

Don't pay cash. Cash tokens cost more than the same trip on a card. On Rapid KL trains (LRT, MRT, Monorail, BRT and the Shah Alam Line) the card to tap is a Touch 'n Go card — the gates don't take bank cards yet. KTM Komuter gates do take contactless Visa, Mastercard and MyDebit cards and Apple Pay, as well as Touch 'n Go.

Prices changed on 1 January 2026, the first change in years. Rapid Kembara only pays off on a busy day: single trips cost a few ringgit each, so it takes a full day of hopping on and off to beat tapping as you go.

Is a monthly pass worth it?

Simple test: a typical rail commute across town is around RM3–5 each way. That's roughly RM6–10 a day, so a full month of weekday commuting is far more than RM50. If you're a Malaysian commuting daily, My50 pays for itself in about a week and everything after is free. Occasional riders should just tap a Touch 'n Go card as they go — no pass needed.

Concessions

  • Students, senior citizens (60+), and OKU (disabled) get concession fares on Rapid KL and KTM with the right registered card.
  • Children below a height/age threshold generally travel free with a paying adult.

KTM Komuter & the airport

KTM Komuter has its own distance-based fares (not covered by the Rapid KL passes) and its own monthly options. The airport trains (KLIA Ekspres / Transit) are a separate premium service — see our KLIA & Changi guide for the cheaper bus and Komuter alternatives.
