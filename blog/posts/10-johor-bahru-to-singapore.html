The Causeway between Johor Bahru and Woodlands is one of the busiest land crossings in the world — ICA counted more than 598,000 travellers on a single day in June 2026. Without a car you have two real choices today: the five-minute KTM Shuttle Tebrau train, or one of the cross-border buses. The new RTS Link train is built but not yet carrying passengers.

{{photo:photo-causeway|The Johor–Singapore Causeway with the Johor Bahru skyline at dusk}}
Before you go: foreigners entering Malaysia submit the free Malaysia Digital Arrival Card (MDAC) within 3 days before arrival; everyone entering Singapore except Singapore citizens, PRs and long-term pass holders submits the free SG Arrival Card (MyICA app or ICA website) within 3 days before arrival. Malaysians need the SG Arrival Card too.

Option 1 — KTM Shuttle Tebrau (train)

A short train from JB Sentral to Woodlands Train Checkpoint, about five minutes across the Strait. You clear Malaysian exit at JB Sentral and Singapore immigration at Woodlands.

Option 2 — cross-border buses

Buses take you to the checkpoint on each side; you get off with your bags to clear immigration, then board the next bus of the same service. Allow for queues at both checkpoints.

Coming next — the RTS Link

The RTS Link is a 4 km shuttle train between Bukit Chagar in JB and Woodlands North, where it connects to the Thomson–East Coast Line ({{link:sg-rail:TEL}}). Each trip takes about five minutes, and immigration for both countries is done once, at the station you leave from.

  • Status: not open. On 2 October 2026 LTA and Malaysia's MOT said passenger service is now targeted for February 2027, subject to final testing, safety certification and approval by both governments.
  • Fare: not announced yet. Ministers have floated an indicative figure, but nothing is official.

We'll add RTS Link times to the live tracker once the service starts.

Beating the queues

  • Into Singapore is heaviest on weekday mornings (about 5–8am); into JB, weekday evenings (about 5–10pm), plus Friday nights, weekends and school or public holidays.
  • On holiday peaks, ICA has warned of waits of up to three hours. Travel early morning or late evening if you can.
  • At the Woodlands and Tuas bus halls you can scan a MyICA QR code instead of your passport — except first-time foreign visitors, who show their passport.
  • Many passports can use Malaysia's autogates at the JB checkpoints once you've submitted the MDAC.
